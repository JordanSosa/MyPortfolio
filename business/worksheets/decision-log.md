# Decision Log

One entry per decision you'd otherwise re-litigate. Five minutes to write,
saves hours of second-guessing — and in a year it's the only record of *why*
the business is shaped the way it is.

Worth an entry: pricing changes, saying no to a feature or a customer,
structure and tax choices, vendor picks, channel bets, anything you argued with
yourself about.

## Standing decisions

Made once, in advance, so they aren't remade under pressure. These are the
policies from `05`, `08` and `11` — write them here and they become answers you
already have rather than judgement calls at the worst moment.

| Decision | Made | Ref |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No feature is built until 3 paying customers ask for the same outcome | ☐ | `05` |
| No bespoke work for a single customer | ☐ | `05` |
| No free plan; 14-day trial with card required | ☐ | `08` |
| No phone support, no live chat | ☐ | `11` |
| Refunds granted without argument inside 30 days | ☐ | `08` |
| No discount to save a churning customer | ☐ | `14` R7 |
| No enterprise procurement — questionnaires, custom MSAs, POs | ☐ | `05` |
| 30% of profit to the tax account, monthly | ☐ | `17` |
| No single channel above 50% of acquisition | ☐ | `18` R16 |

> The three-customer rule is the highest-leverage line in the table. It is what
> stops the product becoming an unmaintainable configuration matrix built by
> whoever emailed most recently.
