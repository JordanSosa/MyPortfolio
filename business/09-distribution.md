# 09 — Distribution

The default assumption of a technical founder is that the product is the hard
part. It is not. **Distribution is the business**, and it is the block that
permanently consumes the largest share of your 10 hours.

## Channels ranked by ongoing hours consumed

The usual ranking is by reach. That is the wrong axis for you. This is ranked
by *hours per customer acquired, in steady state.*

| Channel | Hours/week | Time to work | Compounds? | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SEO / long-tail content | 3–4 | 4–9 months | Yes, strongly | **Primary.** The only channel that keeps working while you sleep |
| Comparison & alternative pages | 1 | 2–4 months | Yes | **High priority.** Highest-intent traffic there is |
| Programmatic / templated pages | 1 (after build) | 3–6 months | Yes | Strong if your data suits it |
| Directories & marketplaces | 0.5 one-off | Immediate | No | Do them once, forget them |
| Partnerships / referrals | 1–2 | 2–6 months | Partly | Good in professional niches |
| Community participation | 2 | 1–3 months | No | Effective early, doesn't scale, stops when you stop |
| Email newsletter | 1–2 | 3+ months | Yes | Best owned asset. Start collecting from day one |
| Paid search | 1 + budget | Immediate | No | Useful to *test* intent; unaffordable to depend on |
| Cold outreach | 5+ | Immediate | No | Do it to get the first 10 customers, then stop |
| Social media | 3–5 | 3+ months | No | Highest hours, most fickle. Avoid as a primary channel |
| Product Hunt / launch sites | 1 one-off | One day | No | A spike, not a channel. Fine, but expect nothing lasting |

## The strategy

**Two channels, worked properly, beat eight worked badly.**

- **Primary: SEO built on genuinely useful content for a narrow audience.**
- **Secondary: whichever of partnerships or community fits your ICP.**
- **Cold outreach for the first ten customers only** — it does not scale and it
  is not meant to. Its purpose is to get you talking to buyers, not to build a
  pipeline.

## SEO for a niche B2B product

You are not competing for "compliance software". You are competing for
questions only your ICP asks, which have low volume and almost no competition,
and which convert at rates a broad keyword never will.

**Content types that earn their keep:**

- [ ] **The definitive guide to the thing you solve.** One long, genuinely
      excellent page, updated quarterly. This is your cornerstone.
- [ ] **"How to X" for every sub-task** in the workflow you serve.
- [ ] **Comparison pages** — "`<Incumbent>` alternatives", "`<A>` vs `<B>`".
      Highest intent, and people searching them have their card out.
- [ ] **Free tools** that solve a slice of the problem without signup — a
      calculator, a checker, a validator. These earn links and rank forever.
- [ ] **Templates and checklists** gated behind an email address.
- [ ] **Programmatic pages** if your data supports it — one page per
      jurisdiction, per category, per date. Only if each page is genuinely
      useful; thin templated pages are worse than nothing.

**Discipline:** one substantial piece per week, forever. Consistency beats
brilliance. Twelve months of one-per-week is 50 pages of compounding surface
area; three brilliant pieces in a burst is nothing.

**Technical basics, done once:** fast pages, sensible titles and meta
descriptions, one H1, internal links between related pages, clean URLs, a
sitemap, structured data where it applies, and no JavaScript requirement to
read the content. You already build fast static sites — this is a strength.

## Finding where your ICP actually is

Before spending an hour on any channel, answer these in writing:

- [ ] What professional association do they belong to? Does it have a
      newsletter, a member directory, a job board, a conference?
- [ ] What newsletter do they read? Can you sponsor it? (Niche B2B newsletter
      sponsorships are often $200–$800 and dramatically undervalued.)
- [ ] What forum, subreddit, Slack, Discord or LinkedIn group do they complain
      in?
- [ ] Who else sells to them without competing with you? Those are your
      referral partners.
- [ ] What software do they already use daily? A directory or marketplace
      listing there is high-intent, one-off work.

## Partnerships in professional niches

For the ICPs in `02`, this is often the fastest channel, because professional
services people ask each other what they use.

- [ ] Identify 10 non-competing suppliers to the same ICP
- [ ] Offer a genuine referral fee — 20% of first-year revenue is standard and
      worth every cent, because referred customers churn less
- [ ] Give them something to send: a one-pager, a discount code, a co-branded
      guide
- [ ] Make it trivially easy — a link, a code, nothing to administer monthly

## The email list

Start collecting from the day the landing page exists, before the product does.

- [ ] An offer worth an email address — a checklist, a template, a calculator,
      not "subscribe to our newsletter"
- [ ] Send something useful monthly, minimum. A list you never email is dead
      within a year
- [ ] Keep it entirely separate from transactional email (see `06`)
- [ ] Comply with the Spam Act: consent, sender identification, and a
      functional unsubscribe on every message. This is Australian law, not a
      nicety

## Things that look like distribution but aren't

- **A big launch.** One day of traffic from people who will never buy.
- **Chasing press.** Journalists do not cover $149/month niche B2B tools.
- **Posting about building in public to other builders.** They are not your
  customers. It feels productive because it generates engagement from an
  audience that will never pay you.
- **Redesigning the landing page** instead of writing the fortieth piece of
  content.

## The steady-state marketing week (4 hours)

| Block | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Write and publish one piece | 2.5 hrs |
| Distribute it — the two communities, the newsletter, the partners | 0.5 hr |
| Reply to comments, answer questions in the ICP's forum | 0.5 hr |
| Review search performance, note what's working | 0.5 hr |

Protect this block. It is the first thing that gets sacrificed to product work
and the last thing you can afford to lose.
