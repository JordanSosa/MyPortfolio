# 04 — Validation Plan

**21 days. Under $250. One question: will a stranger give me money for this
before it exists?**

Everything else — traffic, sign-ups, "interest", encouraging replies — is
noise. People are pleasant. Pleasantness is not revenue.

## The gate

You may start building when **five people who are not your friends have paid a
non-trivial deposit**, or when **three have signed a paid pilot agreement**.

If you reach day 21 without that, you do not build. You go back to `02`. This
rule only works if you commit to it in advance, so commit to it now, in
writing:

> I will not write product code until the gate is met. Signed: ........ Date: ........

## Week 1 — Get specific

**Goal: replace assumptions with a named list and a written problem statement.**

- [ ] Write the ICP in one sentence. Not a category — a describable business.
      *"Australian accounting firms with 3–15 staff who do company secretarial
      work for 50+ client entities and currently track ASIC deadlines in a
      spreadsheet or in CAS 360."*
- [ ] Build a list of **50 named prospects** in a spreadsheet: business name,
      website, one decision-maker, email, source, notes. Public sources only —
      association directories, professional body member lists, LinkedIn,
      Google Maps.
- [ ] Research incumbents. For the top three: pricing page screenshot, feature
      list, and their most common complaint. Mine reviews, forums, and any
      professional community where the ICP talks candidly.
- [ ] Write the problem statement in the customer's own words, quoting at least
      three real complaints you found verbatim.
- [ ] Decide the wedge: the one job the incumbents do badly or not at all.
      Write it as a sentence starting *"Today they have to ..."*.

**Checkpoint:** if you could not find 50 named prospects, the market is too
diffuse. Stop and re-scope.

## Week 2 — Talk to humans

**Goal: 10 conversations. Not surveys — conversations.**

- [ ] Email 30 of the 50. Ask for 15 minutes, offer nothing, sell nothing.
      Short, specific, no attachments. Expect 5–8 replies.

  > Subject: How you track ASIC dates
  >
  > Hi <name> — I'm researching how firms your size handle annual review and
  > lodgement deadlines. Not selling anything; I'm trying to work out whether
  > a problem I keep hearing about is real.
  >
  > Would you have 15 minutes in the next fortnight? Happy to share what I
  > find across all the firms I speak to.
  >
  > <you>

- [ ] Run the calls. Ask about the last time it went wrong, what they use now,
      what they pay, who decides, and what happens if nothing changes. **Do
      not describe your idea until the last two minutes**, and when you do,
      describe the outcome, not the software.
- [ ] Log every call in a single doc: quotes, current tooling, current spend,
      severity 1–5.
- [ ] After call 10, write half a page: is the pain real, urgent, and
      budgeted? Any two of three is enough. One of three is not.

**Cost so far: $0.**

## Week 3 — Ask for money

**Goal: pass the gate.**

- [ ] Build a one-page site. Outcome headline, three-point how-it-works, the
      customer's own words as proof, transparent pricing, and one action.
      You already have the pattern for this in `corp-sec-desk/` — reuse it.
      Half a day, not a week.
- [ ] Put a real price on it. Two tiers. Real numbers.
- [ ] Make the ask, one of:
      - **Founding-member deposit** — $99–$299, credited to the first year,
        refundable on request, delivery date stated in writing.
      - **Paid pilot** — a signed one-page agreement: 3 months at half price,
        starting on a stated date, cancellable any time.
- [ ] Go back to every one of the 10 conversations with the specific ask, plus
      the remaining 20 on the list.
- [ ] Post where the ICP actually is — the professional association forum, the
      LinkedIn group, the industry newsletter. Not Product Hunt; they are not
      there.
- [ ] Optional: $150 of tightly targeted search ads against the highest-intent
      keyword. This measures whether intent exists at all, not whether the ads
      work.

**Cost: ~$20 domain + ~$150 optional ads.**

## Scoring day 21

| Result | Meaning | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5+ deposits, or 3 paid pilots | Real demand, real price | Build. Go to `05`. |
| 1–4 deposits | Something is there; the offer or the price is wrong | One more fortnight, changed offer, same market |
| Warm words, zero money | Pain is real but not urgent or not budgeted | Re-scope to an adjacent, sharper pain |
| No replies at all | Wrong list, wrong channel, or no pain | Back to `02` |

"Warm words, zero money" is the most common and the most dangerous outcome,
because it feels like success. It is not. Treat it as a no.

## What you must have written down by day 21

- [ ] ICP in one sentence
- [ ] 50 named prospects, with contact detail
- [ ] 10 call notes, with verbatim quotes
- [ ] Problem statement in the customer's language
- [ ] Wedge: the one job you do that incumbents don't
- [ ] Price, tested by asking for it
- [ ] Names and emails of everyone who paid, and everyone who nearly did
- [ ] A go / no-go decision with a one-line reason

That last list is the most valuable output of the entire pack. It is also the
part every founder skips.
